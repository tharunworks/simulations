    3. Notes and edge cases

    Why the same player on different devices matters

    Concrete example: Alice is White and logged in on a laptop and a phone. Both game servers think it is White's first turn. A network partition prevents synchronization.

    Her laptop submits 1. e4 to Server A. Her phone submits 1. d4 to Server B. Each move is legal individually from the starting position, so independent servers might acknowledge both.

    When communication returns, both cannot be move 1 in one game. One acknowledged move must be rolled back or rejected after the fact. Turn-based gameplay does not prevent concurrent requests, retries, or multiple sessions.

    Vocabulary: client, partition, operation

    Client: Alice's phone/laptop or Bob's browser.

    Operation: submit a move, fetch current board, read spectator view.

    Partition: a communication failure between servers that may remain running.

    CAP availability: each request received by a nonfailed node eventually completes with a successful response; returning an error does not meet this definition.

    CAP consistency: linearizability: successful operations appear to execute atomically in an order compatible with real-time completion.

    How to prevent conflicting moves

    Use one authoritative game writer, optimistic version checks, idempotency keys and fencing during leadership changes.

    A version check protects against racing writers sharing the same database. It does not prevent independently writable primaries from diverging; that requires controlling leadership and failover.

    PostgreSQL with two async replicas

    The primary commits moves; R1 and R2 replay write-ahead logs asynchronously. A replica can return a stale board even without a partition. During a partition, it may remain stale until it reconnects.

    Asynchronous primary acknowledgment does not prove that either standby has received a move. If the primary dies before shipping it, failover to a lagging replica can lose an acknowledged move. That is a durability/failover problem in addition to stale-read consistency.

    Read from the authoritative primary, or verify a replica's catch-up position, when freshness is required.

    What changes during failover?

    If a standby cannot reach the primary, it cannot tell whether the primary is dead or merely isolated. Promoting the standby without fencing the old primary can create split brain.

    Failover coordination can use a leader lease, fencing and/or a quorum-based controller. If the required authority or replicated state is unavailable, pause move commits instead of accepting conflicting results. Quorum and synchronous replication are distinct design decisions.

    Clock expiry edge case

    Another conflict is a player move versus a server-generated timeout. Different servers could acknowledge both a move and a loss on time. A single authoritative ordering and clock policy are required. Whether the clock pauses during disconnection is a separate product decision.

    MetricWhat it measures
    Conflicting acknowledged movesSafety violations; target zero
    Lost acknowledged movesDurability violations; target zero if promised
    Move acknowledgment p95/p99Coordination cost
    Move submission availabilityRequests served during failures
    Replica replay lag / recovery timeRead staleness and failover exposure

    These are suggested metrics, not benchmark observations.

    Interview: WHAT → WHY → HOW → TRADE-OFF

    WHAT: assign one authoritative writer per game.

    WHY: Alice can submit different moves from two devices while partitioned servers both believe it is her turn.

    HOW: validate versions and legality, commit atomically, deduplicate retries and fence old writers during failover.

    TRADE-OFF: pause or reject moves during some partitions, but permit stale spectator views. Choose independent replication guarantees for acknowledged-move durability.

    What the simulator does and does not model

    These deterministic step-by-step snapshots show a standard chess starting position and the legal alternative first moves e4/d4. They do not implement an entire chess engine, real PostgreSQL, consensus, or a timing-sensitive network.

    In the consistency-preference mode, Server A is assumed to retain authority. A quorum-managed A would also have to stop committing if it lost its required quorum. The AP and CP buttons illustrate behavior per operation; they do not formally classify a production product.
